Home Modifications and Daily Living Aids: Making Home Work for You

Home should be the easiest place in your world — not an obstacle course. For people living with disability in Perth, the right mix of home modifications and daily living aids can transform daily routines from exhausting to effortless, and open up independence that no amount of extra effort could.

Start small: daily living aids

Not everything requires builders. Many of the biggest quality-of-life wins come from small, relatively inexpensive equipment:

  • Bathroom: shower commodes and chairs, grab rails, handheld shower heads, non-slip surfaces.
  • Kitchen: one-handed chopping boards, jar openers, kettle tippers, reachers, adapted cutlery.
  • Bedroom: bed rails, adjustable beds, transfer boards, dressing aids like button hooks and sock aids.
  • Around the home: key turners, door lever adapters, smart-home controls for lights, blinds, air conditioning and doors — genuinely transformative for people with limited hand function.

Bigger changes: home modifications

Where aids are not enough, structural modifications adapt the home itself:

Minor modifications

Grab rails, ramps, handheld showers, widened thresholds, lever taps and improved lighting. These are usually quick to install and make an outsized difference to safety and independence.

Major modifications

Bathroom conversions to hobless, wheel-in showers; widened doorways and corridors; kitchen height adjustments; ceiling hoists; ramped or lift access. These require occupational therapy assessment, detailed quotes and — where NDIS funded — a formal approval process.

How the process usually works

  • Occupational therapy assessment: an OT assesses how you use your home and recommends specific aids or modifications tied to your goals. This report drives everything that follows.
  • Funding: the NDIS can fund assistive technology and home modifications where they are reasonable and necessary. Low-cost aids are often straightforward; major modifications take longer and need solid evidence. Your support coordinator, plan manager or OT can guide the paperwork.
  • Trial before you buy where possible: equipment that looks perfect in a catalogue can feel wrong in real life. Many suppliers and therapy services offer trials — use them.

Think about the whole routine, not just the room

The best assessments look at how your day actually flows: where transfers happen, where fatigue bites, where a carer or support worker needs space to assist safely. A beautifully modified bathroom still fails if the corridor to reach it is too narrow. Support workers who know your routines well can offer valuable practical insights to feed into an OT assessment.

Independence is the goal

Every rail, ramp and gadget is really about the same thing: more of your day under your own control. That is a goal worth investing in properly.

Renting, and other real-world complications

Modifying a home you do not own adds a layer of negotiation. Landlord consent is generally needed for structural changes, though many aids — portable ramps, clamp-on rails, shower chairs, bed levers — require no alteration at all and travel with you when you move. If you are looking at major modifications, it is worth weighing whether the current home can realistically be adapted or whether relocating to a more suitable property is the better long-term investment. An occupational therapist can help assess this honestly, and options like Specialist Disability Accommodation exist for those who qualify under the NDIS.

Technology is moving fast

Smart-home technology deserves special mention because it has quietly become one of the most powerful daily-living tools available. Voice-controlled lights, blinds, door locks, intercoms and air conditioning can restore control over a home environment to people with very limited hand function — often at a fraction of the cost of structural work. Video doorbells add security and independence in answering the door; smart plugs turn ordinary appliances into controllable ones. If technology intimidates you, this is a perfect area to get help setting things up once, properly, and then simply enjoy them.
