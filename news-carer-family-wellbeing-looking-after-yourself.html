Looking After Yourself: Wellbeing for Family Carers

Behind many people living with disability is a partner, parent, sibling or friend quietly doing an enormous amount — coordinating appointments, providing hands-on care, managing the mental load of it all. If that is you, this article is for you. Your wellbeing is not a luxury to attend to after everyone else is sorted. It is the foundation the whole arrangement rests on.

The honest truth about carer strain

Caring can be deeply meaningful and genuinely exhausting at the same time. Broken sleep, physical strain, reduced work hours, shrinking social circles and the constant low hum of responsibility all add up. Many carers do not notice the toll until they are well past their limits — partly because they never stop long enough to check.

Practical ways to protect your wellbeing

Use respite without guilt

Respite — whether a few hours a week of in-home support or longer planned breaks — is not abandonment. It is maintenance. The person you care for benefits from a rested, present version of you, and often values time with other people too. NDIS plans can fund supports that give family carers a genuine break; ask about it at the next plan review.

Keep one thing that is yours

A morning swim, a weekly coffee with friends, a hobby, exercise — protect at least one regular commitment that has nothing to do with caring. It anchors your identity beyond the caring role.

Share the load deliberately

Make a list of everything you do in a week. Then ask honestly: which of these could someone else do? Professional support workers can take on personal care, transfers, overnight support and transport — often the tasks that strain bodies and relationships the most. Handing over the physical tasks can give you back the relationship: partner instead of nurse, parent instead of coordinator.

Watch your own health signals

Persistent exhaustion, irritability, low mood, aches from lifting, or dreading each day are signals, not character flaws. Speak to your own GP — carers deserve healthcare too, and support is available. Carer support organisations in WA also offer counselling, peer groups and advice.

Building an arrangement that lasts years, not months

Sustainable care is a team sport. The strongest arrangements we see in Perth combine family love with professional support: family providing connection and continuity, trained workers providing skilled hands and reliable relief. Neither replaces the other.

You are allowed to ask for help

The conversations worth having early

Many carer arrangements drift into place during a crisis and are never consciously designed. It is worth sitting down — ideally before exhaustion forces the issue — and talking honestly as a family about what is sustainable. Which parts of care does the person want family involved in, and which would they genuinely prefer a professional to handle? Personal care in particular can strain relationships in both directions; plenty of people would rather a trained worker assist with showering so their partner remains simply their partner. These conversations can feel awkward, but they almost always land better as planning than as a breakdown response.

Planning for the unexpected

Every caring arrangement needs a what-if plan: what happens if you are suddenly unwell, injured or called away? Having a provider who already knows the person, their routines and their home means an emergency does not become a crisis. Some families build this in deliberately — a regular weekly shift with a professional team, even when family could technically cover it — precisely so backup is always warm, familiar and ready. Think of it as insurance that also happens to give you a night off.
